Get the door. It's the liquor store.

Drizly is an online ordering and delivery platform that facilitates the delivery of alcohol from local retailers via its mobile app or website. I joined the company as the founding designer, with the opportunity to design ALL the things. It was a riot.
First things first. We needed a website.

Drizly dot com, a near constant evolution.

The initial offering was propped up inside of a month, with minimal shopping capabilities, but within the state boundaries of New York and Boston, you could purchase and have alcohol delivered to your doorstep. We tried a lot of things after that, also.

We need a website

A proof of concept really, the first live Drizly e-commerce site was designed in the passenger seat of my car while my Dad drove/helped move me from Austin back to Boston. We needed a functional website that could complete a transaction/deliver in order to raise our first angel round of capital.

Okay, now let's rebrand

With some cash and breathing room in our pocket, we were able to work with a local agency to help us with our branding and voice, which would ultimately end solidifying our logo and helping us work through early marketing, web, and social media assets.

Catalog landing page

We were operating in an unknown, grey area of online sales. Did we need an age ID landing page? Do we collect address first? What are the best practices for this new world of alcohol purchasing? We tried EVERYTHING.

Iterate, release, test, repeat

The first few months of Drizly started the same way: check google analytics. Where did the traffic drop off? What is converting? Are we getting addresses? How can we improve this entirely unique and new shopping experience that is only available in two states? Oh also, we've sold $150k worth of banner ads...
Next, we needed a flagship iPhone App.

Designed. Built. Shipped in 3 months.

In order to really make a splash while fundraising for our first official round, we needed a flagship iOS app. Myself and our newly hired iOS developer worked tirelessly to build the best app possible as quickly as possible. We launched with an absolutely feature rich V1, complete with a robust profile section, a "Domino's" style deliver tracker, Favorites, Promo Code tracking, and even an "upsell" section for checkout.
Drizly for iPhone hit the store on a Wednesday. The next Wednesday, we secured $5 million in funding.
Rocket Ship Growth

Build all the things.

I built out the design team quickly, and organized the product into 5 verticals (iOS, Web, Mobile Web, Android, Marketing) with a designer for each (two on the marketing side). We were focused on a million things all at once, with each vertical requiring it's own unique design solutions.

Brand Pages

I designed and shipped a CMS driven, customizable, brand page. Just like that, brands like Miller and Corona were able to own, manage, and skin their own category landing page on drizly.com. Every marketing campaign, sponsored tweet, and influencer driven instagram post could now be actionable, monetizable, and purchase driven. It sold itself, and we sold A TON of them.

Take a look at a full Miller Brand Page template>

Faceted Search + Filtering

We expanded our delivery offerings almost daily. As we onboarded more and more stores, users no longer had to just shop from a single liquor store. Certain stores also started to offer shipping, delivery scheduling, and even in-store pickup. We immediately needed filtering and faceted search in order to handle the ever expanding shopping experience.

SEO. Recipe Pages. Gifting.

Previous to Drizly, simple web searches like "Heineken", or "Margarita" we're fairly low value. We took advantage of these long tail search terms, and dirt cheap google ad prices to start buying them up and directly the traffic at tailored landing pages within Drizly.com where we could make these searches actionable and purchasable. Designed on the framework of the brand pages, users could land on a recipe page for a Margarita, and be able to instantly purchase all the ingredients necessary.


We put a lot of thought and effort into the Gifting experience also. Not only was it a fantastic opportunity for the Holidays, but gifting gave us a chance to allow users, that previously were outside of a service area, the ability to use Drizly to deliver to someone that was able to receive it.

Solving experience problems with store integration.

Drizly, for stores.

Due to the nature of state liquor laws surrounding delivery, the store partners we used had to hire their own delivery drivers. This complicated the customer experience, and left us little room to be helpful when it came to wrong orders, poor deliveries, or inaccurate store inventories. I spent months working with store partners to design an experience tailored for them, that would entice them to integrate their point of sale systems with a Drizly api that would allow us to better track and manage which products they had available. We also wanted to surface potential missed opportunities based on what they were carrying, and what was selling out in the market.
